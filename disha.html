A wayfinding system that helps visitors find their departments through a colour-coded ceiling path, a simple kiosk interaction and audio instructions without relying on text heavy signage or repeatedly asking for directions.

Problem

Setting: Large public hospital campus, multiple blocks, floors, corridors, departments

Primary user: First-time rural visitor, semi-literate, no smartphone, often accompanied by family

First-time visitors often struggle to locate departments across multiple blocks, floors and corridors. With unclear directions and limited guidance, they end up asking strangers, following other patients or looking for security staff.

Task: Navigate from entry gate to a specific department, lab, or pharmacy in a different block

Current fallback: Ask a stranger, follow another patient, find a security guard, or give up

Discovery Phase

Affinity Mapping

Information breakdown in the navigation system

Signs assume English literacy. Announcements are in Telugu only. Colour lines get lost in crowds. Kiosks are hidden in corners.

Socially mediated navigation

Most people are directed by other patients or security staff. They reconfirm at every turn, so finding a room becomes an anxious loop.

Spatial disorientation and navigation stress

Long, repetitive corridors. Junctions cause wrong turns even on repeat visits. Every wrong turn adds to staff workload.

Developing Phase

Design Rationale

At the hospital, visitors often followed others when unsure of the route. Studies show people imitate those ahead. We created a colour-coded ceiling path, letting visitors follow a clear visual trail instead of others.

System Overview

A hospital wayfinding system that combines kiosk-based interaction with continuous colour-coded navigation.

01. Wall-mounted Kiosk Placed at key locations such as entrances, lobbies and lift areas. Visitors scan their hospital card to receive department information and route direction

02. Colour-coded Ceiling Path A continuous colour-coded path guides visitors through corridors and junctions towards their destination.
