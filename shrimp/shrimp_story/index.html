01 / 1970s–1980s

It started small.

Shrimp farming began as small-scale experiments along Andhra Pradesh’s coast. By the late 1980s, hatcheries emerged, making shrimp seed more accessible to farmers.

02 / Early–mid 1990s

Then came the shrimp boom.

Rising export demand made black tiger shrimp highly profitable. Farmers across coastal Andhra Pradesh began converting agricultural land into ponds, rapidly expanding cultivation.

03 / Late 1990s–2000s

But the boom didn’t last.

White Spot Syndrome Virus (WSSV) devastated farms. Dense stocking, poor biosecurity and infected seed helped the disease spread, causing major losses.

04 / 2009 onwards

A new shrimp changed everything.

Farmers adopted vannamei shrimp, introduced commercially in 2009. Faster growth, higher stocking potential and specific pathogen-free seed helped the industry boom again.

Across the globe

In 2024–25 India shipped 7.4 lakh tonnes of frozen shrimp, most of it farmed in Andhra Pradesh. Six markets took nine-tenths of it.
